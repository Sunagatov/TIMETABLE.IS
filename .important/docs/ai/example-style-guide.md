# Memora example style guide

Use this guide when generating example sentences for vocabulary enrichment.

The goal is not just to produce examples.  
The goal is to produce examples that are **natural, useful, readable, and non-templated**.

---

## Core output rule

Write **exactly 3** example sentences per word or phrase **when enrichment is needed**.

If a word already has 3 strong examples, leave it alone.

---

## Main rules

- make each sentence sound natural in English
- keep the level around B1 to C1
- use the target word or phrase naturally in every sentence
- keep examples concise, concrete, and easy to read
- prefer everyday situations when they fit the word
- for Memora, favor household, routine, repair, cleaning, tools, appliance, and daily-life contexts when relevant
- keep the three examples varied enough that they do not read like templates
- vary sentence structure
- avoid Russian, translations, glosses, or commentary inside the examples
- if the word has multiple senses, use the sense that best fits the topic and stay consistent
- prefer model-written examples over deterministic filler
- if existing examples are already strong and varied, keep them

---

## Quality bar

A good Memora example set should feel like something a careful teacher or native speaker would actually write, not something machine-generated from a rigid pattern.

### Good qualities

- natural wording
- varied sentence openings
- varied subjects
- realistic context
- direct relevance to the word/topic
- no unnecessary complexity
- no repetitive grammar skeleton

### Bad qualities

- template-like repetition
- repeated sentence stems
- repeated subjects without reason
- robotic “dictionary example” tone
- awkward over-formality
- examples that merely force the target word into the sentence

---

## Completion threshold

Treat 3 strong example sentences as the **done** threshold.

That means:

- do not keep rewriting already-good examples
- do not rewrite just to make counts uniform
- do not replace good examples with flatter ones

---

## Topic-awareness rule

If a word has multiple senses, choose the sense that best fits the current topic.

Do not mix unrelated senses across the 3 examples unless the task explicitly requires it.

---

## Variation rule

Across the 3 examples, vary at least some of:

- subject
- sentence opening
- context
- verb structure
- time reference
- tone

The goal is to avoid a templated feel.

---

## Examples of what to avoid

### Too templated
- I cleaned the sink before breakfast.
- I cleaned the mirror before breakfast.
- I cleaned the stove before breakfast.

### Better
- I cleaned the sink after washing the dishes.
- She wiped the mirror so the bathroom looked brighter.
- We scrubbed the stove after the soup boiled over.

---

## Keep / replace rule

### Keep existing examples if they are:
- natural
- varied
- relevant
- readable
- already complete enough

### Replace existing examples only if they are:
- repetitive
- awkward
- templated
- unnatural
- irrelevant to the topic
- too weak to help learning

---

## Final check before accepting an example set

Ask:

1. Does each sentence use the target word naturally?
2. Do the 3 examples feel different enough?
3. Would a learner understand them without extra commentary?
4. Do they sound like real English rather than generated filler?

If not, improve them.
